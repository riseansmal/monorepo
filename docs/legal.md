Independent clients choose terms for their own additions while retaining applicable upstream notices.

Third-party dependencies, bundled third-party code, examples, fixtures and assets retain their own licenses. The MIT grant does not relicense them or establish their ownership. Dependencies are fetched from public registries rather than vendored in this source tree. Preserve their license and attribution files when packaging software; do not assume a package's top-level license covers all embedded native libraries.

Review exact locked versions and the files actually shipped for each distribution. Optional generated projects can introduce additional obligations: Sharp's libvips binaries carry LGPL terms, Lightning CSS and several Python dependencies use MPL terms, and caniuse-lite data uses CC-BY terms. Source availability, linking, attribution and notices must be evaluated for the chosen packaging. These examples are not an exhaustive dependency inventory or permission to ignore other licenses.

New external code and assets must record their source, copyright holder, license and required notices before inclusion. Flag unclear ownership and resolve it with the rights holder before distribution. Never remove a required notice to satisfy a naming policy. Model weights and hosted services have separate terms and are not supplied by monorepo.

Local validation does not establish redistribution rights or authorize publishing. Usage does not include a support or service-level commitment.
